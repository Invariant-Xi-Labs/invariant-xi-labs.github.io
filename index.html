§ 1   SCOPE

What this lab does

I-XI Labs is a single-operator research practice working on the problem of stating a security property precisely enough that a machine can check it — and then building the machinery that checks it.

The work spans three areas that share that spine: detection engineering validated against adversarial input rather than against a rule count; platform security research on macOS and Apple Silicon; and formal assurance methodology — the vocabulary and structure for saying what a system has actually been shown to do, as distinct from what its documentation claims.

The practice is architectural and defensive. Adversarial technique is used to validate controls and to define vulnerability classes formally, not as an offering.

§ 2   METHOD

Assurance states

Most security documentation conflates three different things: a control that runs, a control that is written down, and a control that someone intends to build. The lab keeps them separate, and every claim it publishes carries one of these markers.

  • EnforcedA running mechanical check verifies this. The check is named, it executes, and its failure is observable. This is the only state that constitutes evidence.
  • DeclaredAsserted, not verified. The property is specified and may well hold, but nothing mechanically confirms it. A policy document produces this state, never the one above.
  • OpenUnresolved. The question is stated and the evidence that would settle it is named. Naming a risk is not mitigating it, and this marker says so explicitly.
